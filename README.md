# After the Revolution: A Data Story of Tunisia (2000–2023)

A data analysis project exploring Tunisia's economic and social trajectory over two decades, using World Bank indicators.

## What this project covers

Tunisia entered the 2000s as one of Africa's most stable economies. This notebook traces what happened across four key periods:

- **Pre-revolution (2000–2010)** — steady growth, but fragile foundations
- **The revolution (2011–2013)** — political transition and its economic cost
- **The terrorism crisis (2014–2016)** — how the Bardo and Sousse attacks collapsed tourism and GDP
- **The economic spiral (2016–2023)** — IMF loan, dinar devaluation, and rising inflation

## Key findings

- Tunisia's GDP per capita peaked in 2014 then fell sharply following the 2015 terrorist attacks
- Tourism revenue dropped significantly after Bardo and Sousse — and the recovery was misleading, driven by weaker-currency visitors
- Military spending hit an all-time high post-2015 as security replaced economic recovery as the government's priority
- The 2016 IMF loan and exchange rate liberalization directly contributed to the inflation spike that followed
